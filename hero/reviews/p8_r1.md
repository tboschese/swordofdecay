## On your reading

You are **half right, and the half you got wrong is the actual diagnosis.**

Wrong: the background is not bright. 100% of it falls in the bottom luminance
decile; not one pixel exceeds lum 0.05. By value it is darker than most
shipped grim-fantasy grounds.

Right: it reads like a mobile-MMO splash anyway. The cause is not brightness,
it is that a mean HSV saturation of 0.876 across a smooth two-point hue ramp is
what a colour gel looks like, not what dark air looks like. At scotopic levels
the eye reads relative chroma, not absolute luminance, so a field where B is
7x R and G with zero variation is perceived as *coloured*, however dark it
measures. You saw "bright and saturated" because saturation at low luminance
is read as glow. Your instinct was correct; the mechanism you assigned to it
was not. So the answer to "background chroma, tonemap shoulder, saturation
stage, or vignette" is: **background chroma, and none of the other three.**

## Interrogating the specific claims

**"Blue is only 7.2% of luminance weight so it stays deep by measurement while
reading as lit air."** This is gaming the metric, and the greyscale render is
the proof. The reasoning is correct about the *measurement* and wrong about the
*eye*. `bg corner lum 0.0122` passes because 0.0122 delivered almost entirely
through the 0.0722 blue coefficient means B ≈ 0.17 linear ≈ 45/255 on screen —
a plainly visible blue, not black. The author found the one channel the metric
under-weights and put the entire background in it. It satisfies `measure.mjs`
and fails rubric item 2, which asks for value structure and for separation by
value *and* temperature. There is no temperature separation either: the blade is
cool-white on a saturated cool field, so it separates by value only. The only
warm note in the frame is the guard's rust, which sits *below* the focal point
and therefore fights the read order instead of supporting it.

**The shoulder/lighting coupling** (line 250) is fragile and the responsibility
is in the wrong module:

```glsl
float lh = dot(c, vec3(0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722));
c *= 1.0 - 0.36*smoothstep(0.20, 0.86, lh)*(1.0 - smoothstep(0.86, 0.99, lh));
```

This is a *display-referred* multiplier keyed to absolute post-ACES, post-clamp
luminance with hardcoded breakpoints. It is monotonic, so it will not invert —
but between lh 0.86 and 0.99 it applies a ~3.4x local gain over a 0.13-wide
band, which is a hard local-contrast wall that will ring around specular cores
as soon as anything lands in it. Worse, the coupling is *inverted*: if p7 drops
absolute intensity, the blade's lit mass slides from ~0.7 down toward ~0.5,
which is deeper into the compression trough and never reaches the 0.86 release —
so a **dimmer** rig gets crushed **harder** and loses its rim protection
entirely. Correct division of responsibility: p7 owns scene-referred radiance
only. p8 owns a single explicit scene-referred exposure normalisation applied
*before* the tonemap, then a monotonic tonemap with a smooth shoulder, and
nothing keyed to display values after it. Then p7 can halve its output and p8's
grade is unchanged. Do not fix this by re-tuning the constants; fix it by
deleting the display-referred dip and moving the level control upstream of ACES.

**Ground contact.** There is none, and it is not an aesthetic oversight, it is
a hardcoded number and an architectural choice:

```glsl
const float P8_FLOOR_Y = -1.02;
vec2 p8_extent(){ Dims D = dims(); return vec2(-(D.gripLen + 0.17), D.bladeLen); }
```

`dims().gripLen = 0.44`, so the underside of the pommel is at y = **-0.61**.
The floor is at **-1.02**. The weapon hovers 0.41 world units — 24% of blade
length — above the ground. Every other framing constant in this file derives
from `dims()`; this one does not, and it is the one that decides whether the
sword touches anything. Second: the floor is evaluated in `backgroundCol`, i.e.
the raymarcher's *miss* path. It is analytic and unoccluded. `map()` and
`softShadow` cannot reach it, so a contact shadow is not merely absent, it is
impossible as currently structured. Either raise the plane to -0.61 and trace an
explicit shadow ray from the floor point back toward the key light, or fake it
with an SDF-distance-modulated darkening around `fp.xz` — but something has to
occlude that plane or the sword will float forever.

**Is the roll a gimmick?** No — `principal axis -73.3 deg` is a genuine
diagonal and the 64px thumbnail reads cleanly as a sword. The roll is the best
decision in the file. The *framing around it* is the problem, and the header
comment overclaims: "pushed off the dead centre of the frame". `P8_SHIFT =
vec2(0.058, 0.002)`. The y term is zero, and the measurement confirms it —
bbox y[56..394] in a 450px frame is a 56px top margin and a 56px bottom margin,
**dead centre to the pixel**. Horizontally it is offset (left margin 77, right
137), which is fine. But the halo is centred at `s = (0.020, 0.020)` and the
shaft is only 14 deg off the blade's own lean, so both background elements are
concentric with the subject. A diagonal object inside a radially symmetric
bullseye reads as a specimen on a light table, not as a composed shot. The roll
is doing real work; the staging cancels it.

**Banding.** With `gw = smoothstep(0.03, 0.22, l)`, the grain is gated off
below l = 0.03 — and the background sits at l ≈ 0.02. The one region of the
image with the fewest available quantisation levels is the one region that
receives no dither. Hence 13 distinct blue values over 450 rows and 33px
constant-value plateaus. This is a real contouring artifact, but it is a
consequence of the main problem, not a separate one: once the field is not a
smooth single-channel ramp it largely goes away. Lowering the grain gate to
~0.005 is a two-character insurance policy regardless.

## Biggest gap

**The background is authored entirely in chroma and not at all in luminance,
so in value terms the weapon floats in a featureless void.** Background mean
HSV saturation is 0.876 against the subject's 0.352 — the environment is 2.5x
more colourful than the hero object, which is backwards from every shipped
loot-reveal, where the ground is a desaturated near-black and the *weapon* owns
the colour. Desaturate the render and the entire staged environment — shaft,
halo, floor, embers — disappears. That is why it reads as a fantasy poster with
a coloured gel behind it rather than as a grim dungeon.

Fix, in one intent: **stop paying for the environment in hue and start paying
for it in value.**

1. Neutralise the palette. Every background constant currently runs R and G at
   ~15-20% of B. Bring them to ~50-65% — a cold *slate/ash*, not a blue. E.g.
   the base vault `vec3(0.00110, 0.00145, 0.00520)` becomes roughly
   `vec3(0.00280, 0.00320, 0.00520)`; same treatment for the halo and shaft
   terms. Target: **background mean HSV saturation ≤ 0.35**, below the
   subject's. Sanity-check by rendering, desaturating, and confirming the image
   still looks essentially the same.
2. Spend the luminance that frees up on structure the eye can actually see.
   Raise `P8_FLOOR_Y` to `p8_extent().x` so the plane meets the pommel, push the
   stone base from ~0.010 to ~0.030-0.040 linear, and let the horizon carry a
   real value break — the ground should sit measurably *below* the air above it,
   not at the same 0.023 mean. Then occlude it: a contact shadow under the
   pommel, however cheaply faked, is what converts "sword in front of a
   gradient" into "sword standing in a room."
3. Keep `bg corner lum ≤ 0.02` so the existing metric still passes. It will —
   you are moving light around inside the frame, not adding it.

Do this and nothing else this round. The tonemap shoulder and the vignette are
not the problem; leave them until p7's level is settled, then delete the
display-referred dip rather than re-tuning it.

## What is already working

The 15-degree roll is the right call and should not be touched — `-73.3 deg`
principal axis with 75.3% height fill and a clean 64px thumbnail read is a
genuinely good hero framing, and deriving it from `dims()` was the correct
engineering decision (`P8_FLOOR_Y` is the one place that discipline lapsed).
The sRGB encode you added is correct and is the reason the value spread is
sane at 51/36/13 instead of crushed. The warm-biased halation on the bloom and
the mid-weighted grain gate are both the right *ideas*; only the gate threshold
needs moving.
