# p7_lighting — round 1 — FAIL

## What I measured

`hero/out/crit7_beauty.png` (360x450 ss1 mode0 iso0):

```
subject lum     min 0.047 mean 0.370 max 0.980
value spread    shadow(0-.3) 51.1%  mid(.3-.7) 35.8%  high(.7-1) 13.1%
histogram        26  14  11   5   3   4  24  13   0   0   (deciles 0..1)
spec breakup    rms 0.100 of mean, 61 reversals over 160 rows
```

The three-bucket spread is the number that was tuned to, and it is the number
that is lying. Read the deciles instead:

- Deciles 3,4,5 (0.3-0.6) hold **12%** combined. That is a *valley*.
- Deciles 6,7 (0.6-0.8) hold **37%**. That is a *plateau*.
- Deciles 8,9 (0.8-1.0) hold **0%**. There are no highlights at all.

`max 0.980` is a handful of pixels; the top two deciles round to zero. So the
image is bimodal — a dark background-adjacent mass and a broad bright slab —
with no connective midtone gradient and no specular tail. A shipped hero shot
has the opposite shape: a fat low/mid mass, a smooth falloff, and a thin
persistent tail into 0.9-1.0 where the hot speculars live. The 13.1% "high"
bucket is not highlights. It is the bottom edge of the plateau.

## What I see

The blade is a broad, near-uniform pale slab. It reads as polished aluminium or
a chrome prop, not forged steel — and critically, *both* visible bevels are
roughly equally bright, so the cross-section never turns away from the light.
There is no dark side to this metal anywhere. The slot-wall banding the module
was built for is present but sits entirely inside the 0.6-0.8 plateau, so it
modulates a bright slab rather than carving light out of a dark one. The hilt
(orange oxide, blue-grey grip, green gem) is the only place with real value
range, which inverts the composition: the focal point should be the blade, and
instead the blade is the flattest region in the frame. The overall impression
matches the brief's failure mode exactly — clean, bright, saturated, mobile
splash-screen, not Path of Exile.

**Your read is correct, and it is a lighting problem. Here is how I can tell it
is not the material or the grade.**

1. **It is not p2 (blade material).** The clay pass (`--mode 1`) substitutes
   `albedo 0.42 / rough 0.55 / metal 0.0` in `core_main.glsl:30-36`, bypassing
   `shadeBlade` entirely. The clay render is *also* a blown near-white form with
   the shading gradient almost completely compressed out. Same lighting, no p2,
   same failure. p2 is exonerated by construction.

2. **p2 is in fact already darker than real steel.** `p2_blade_material.glsl:18`
   sets `base = vec3(0.185, 0.196, 0.214)`, reaching at most ~0.31 on the honed
   regions (line 26). Physical polished steel F0 is ~0.56. The material author
   handed you deliberately dark forged steel, *half* the reflectance of real
   steel. A metal has no diffuse term — its entire value is `envRadiance x DFG`.
   For an F0 of 0.20 to render as white, the environment it reflects must be
   roughly **5x above the white point**. That is arithmetic, and it points at
   exactly one module.

3. **It is not p8 (grade).** The sRGB encode at `p8_presentation.glsl:261` is
   the correct standard curve, and the exposure trim at line 222 is `1.05` with
   the comment "the light rig sets the level." The tell is line 250: a **-36%
   band compressor** across 0.20-0.86, whose own comment says "ACES alone leaves
   the lit face of the blade as one bright slab; this bends the upper-mids back
   down." That is not a grade decision. That is a **patch for this module**, and
   it is the direct cause of the 0.6-0.8 plateau: with the dip in place, every
   pre-tonemap linear value from roughly 1.2 to 6.0 lands in the same narrow
   output band. Your "two errors cancelling" hypothesis is confirmed, and this
   line is the second error.

   (The saturated navy background *is* p8's `backgroundCol` and is a fair
   separate criticism of p8. The blade's whiteness is not.)

## Biggest gap

**The rig's absolute light energy is roughly 3 stops too hot, so the blade's
entire lit face sits above the tonemap's shoulder where value differences stop
existing. Cut every emitter in the module by ~10x.**

The chain, traced numerically on the blade flat at `rough ~0.20`:

- `blur = 0.010 + 0.04*0.95 = 0.048` → `crisp = 1 - 0.048*3.1 = 0.85`
- slot wall peak (line 132) `= 0.22 + 13.0*0.85 = **11.3**`
- `dfg` at NoV~0.6, f0=0.20: `0.20*0.9 + 0.03 = 0.21`
- IBL contribution in-slot `= 11.3 * 0.21 = **2.37** pre-tonemap linear`
- ACES(2.37) = 0.836 → shoulder dip x0.64 = 0.535 → sRGB = **0.76**

0.76 is the plateau in the histogram. The measurement and the source agree to
two digits. The dark "pier" between slots computes to ~0.027 pre-tonemap and
*should* render near-black — but the direct lights and tubes are over-driven by
the same factor and fill it back in, which is why the blade has no dark side.
That is the mechanism behind both symptoms at once: blown bands, and no blacks.

**The target.** For dark-fantasy steel you want the dominant flat mass at
display 0.06-0.18, the slot bands at 0.35-0.55, and *only* the edge rim and the
hot-core specular reaching 0.85-1.0. Working display 0.45 backwards:
`srgb^-1(0.45) = 0.17` → undo shoulder → ~0.21 → `ACES^-1(0.21) = **0.22**`
pre-tonemap. You are at 2.37. **The cut is ~10.8x, about 3.4 stops.**

**How much of that is the gamma bug you asked me to quantify.** Pre-fix, the
tonemapped linear was displayed raw. Tuning to a blade mass of ~0.5 displayed
meant a tonemapped 0.5, i.e. pre-tonemap ~0.75. Post-fix, restoring display 0.5
needs tonemapped `srgb^-1(0.5) = 0.214`, i.e. pre-tonemap ~0.30.
**The display-encoding fix alone accounts for a 2.5x cut (1.3 stops).** The
remaining ~4x is genuine over-drive that predates it — the `13.0` wall
multiplier (132), `42.0` hot core (141), `14.0` kicker (145), and the `6.0`/`8.0`
key and rim (285, 292) are simply too hot on their own terms. So: yes, bring the
level down, and no, undoing the gamma compensation is not sufficient — it gets
you a third of the way.

**Concretely:** scale every radiance constant in `envRadiance` and every
`kCol/rCol/tCol/fCol` and tube colour in `lightSurface` by ~0.10 as a single
global first move, then re-key upward only on the *hot core* card (141) and the
edge-rim path so the top two deciles actually get populated. Do not re-key the
fill. The value structure you want comes from the slot/pier *contrast*, which is
currently ~400:1 in the environment and is being thrown away by exposure — you
already built the right rig, you are just running it with the dimmer welded
open. Then tell p8's owner that line 250 can be retired; the correct check is
that disabling that dip should barely change the image once your level is right.
Today it would blow the blade to paper white.

**One structural note, not a second ask.** The module's comments treat
`envRadiance(dir, rough)`'s lack of a position argument as a constraint. It is
not one. `grep` shows `envRadiance` is called from exactly two places, both
inside this file (lines 330 and 335); `core_head.glsl:172` only forward-declares
it. Nothing in the harness core calls it. You are entirely free to write
`p7_envAt(vec3 p, vec3 dir, float rough)` and call that internally, keeping the
contract function as a thin wrapper. Parallax on the slot wall — making the
reflected bands shift with position along the blade rather than being purely
directional — is available whenever you want it. Do not cite the signature as a
reason it cannot be done.

## What is already working

The rig's *structure* is genuinely good and should survive the exposure cut
intact: the periodic slot wall is the correct instinct for a near-flat polished
face, the Karis representative-point tubes give real travelling highlights, and
the finite angular radius on every source is the right call. The spec-breakup
measurement (rms 0.100, 61 reversals over 160 rows) sits squarely in the
"forged steel" band the tool defines, and the warm-ember-below / cold-kicker-
behind temperature split is a proper studio read that will only get better once
it is not competing with a blown midtone. The problem is the level, not the
design — do not tear the rig out.
