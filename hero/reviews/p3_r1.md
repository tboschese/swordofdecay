## What I see

The blade above the ricasso is completely pristine — 0.0% oxide across the top
seven of fourteen bands, i.e. the whole upper half of the weapon. Everything
from the ricasso down is a single flat, bright, terracotta-orange field over the
hooks, guard arms and collar; it reads as orange paint applied to a zone, not as
a material that grew there. The transition is not staged: it goes 0% → 0% → 10%
→ 15% → 56% → 85% across four bands, so five of the six claimed stages have to
happen inside roughly 11% of the weapon's length, and the first stage — the
discoloured halo that is supposed to reach far up clean steel — produces
literally zero warm-shifted pixels on the blade. In the 620px silhouette the
blade and guard outlines are dead-clean straight lines; there is no visible
bite anywhere, so with `--mode 2` now correctly including `rotDisplace`, the
displacement amplitude is confirmed to be below the threshold of registering at
all. And it reads as rust, not as decay: nothing advances, nothing has a front,
nothing is wet next to something dry. The sparse emissive in the fissures does
not appear in the frame at any scale I rendered.

Your three suspicions are all confirmed by the numbers. Your own stated weakness
— blobby flake outlines with no thickness — is real but is **not** the biggest
problem, and I explain why below.

## Biggest gap

**The corroded region carries all of its information in chroma and almost none
in value, so it renders as a flat coloured field rather than a crusty
three-dimensional surface.**

The measured signature is unambiguous: saturation median 0.698 across a
6.3-degree hue IQR, against a luminance IQR of only 0.131. Note what that
combination means — the oxide is not actually *bright* (median lum 0.234, which
is darker than the mean subject luminance of 0.370). It reads loud purely
because it is the single most saturated thing in a navy-and-steel frame. You
have a dark, uniform, extremely chromatic mass. Real corrosion is the opposite:
its chroma is modest and it reads almost entirely through *value* — near-black
pit floors, mid crust, and pale powdery efflorescence on the high points, often
spanning 0.03 to 0.55 luminance within a single square inch. Chroma is nearly
incidental.

This one defect is what hides everything else you built. The flake plates, the
crater floors, the plate edges, the blight — none of them are visible in the
render, and sharpening their outlines will not make them visible, because a
flake separated from its substrate by 0.05 luminance inside a 0.70-saturation
orange field is invisible regardless of how crisp its silhouette is. Fix value
structure first and your flake work becomes legible for free; fix flake
thickness first and you will have spent a round on detail nobody can see. Same
argument retires the coverage complaint in the right order: pushing this oxide
up the blade to fix the 0%-for-seven-bands problem would make the entire sword
traffic-cone orange, so coverage is *blocked* until chroma comes down. And it is
why this reads as rust rather than rot — a spreading biological front is
communicated by a dark necrotic core grading out to a pale advancing rim, which
is a *value* gradient. You cannot express it in a material that has 0.13 of
value range.

Concretely, in `p3_rot.glsl`:

1. **Cut the albedo chroma ratio.** `cRust = vec3(0.155, 0.058, 0.024)` is
   saturation 0.845 and `cFresh = vec3(0.400, 0.150, 0.034)` is 0.915. Raise the
   blue channel and drop red — something near `vec3(0.085, 0.052, 0.038)` for
   the bulk oxide. Target a *rendered* saturation median of 0.30–0.40 with p95
   around 0.60. Keep `cFresh` as a genuine accent used on well under 10% of the
   oxide area; right now `mix(oxide, cFresh*0.80, smoothstep(0.46, 0.22, age))`
   reaches a large fraction of it.
2. **Spend the freed budget on value range.** Target a rendered oxide luminance
   IQR near 0.35, up from 0.131. Your `cOld*0.30` pit-floor albedo
   (≈0.008, 0.006, 0.005) is the right colour, but `craterFloor*0.72` and
   `pitFloor*0.55` never let it land — drive those masks to full strength so the
   floors render at 0.02–0.05. Then add the missing top end: a near-neutral
   (sat < 0.15) pale dry bloom at 0.45–0.55 on the crust high points. Dark floor
   plus pale bloom is what will finally reveal your flake plates.
3. **Decorrelate hue from `age`.** Both hue and value currently ride the same
   `age` field, which is why the distribution collapsed to one hue. Drive hue
   from a separate low-frequency field so the median lands near 30 degrees with
   an IQR spanning roughly 15–50, and raise `blightM*0.60` until the olive
   actually reaches more than 0.43% of the frame.

Once the material reads at 0.30 saturation with 0.35 of value range, expanding
coverage up the blade and increasing the displacement amplitude for silhouette
bite both become safe — and both are round-2 work, not this round's.

## What is already working

The moisture logic itself is sound and visible: the corrosion genuinely pools
in the concave seams around the hooks and the guard collar, climbs from the
hilt, and spares the wiped areas — the *placement* is believable and is not a
uniform noise overlay, which is the part most rot shaders get wrong. The
displacement-sign fix is clearly correct in the shading (the pitting reads as
recessed, not as blisters). And the per-material split is right in principle —
the leather grip staining and moulding rather than pitting is the correct
behaviour, keep it. Do not throw out the mask architecture; it is only the
colour and value response layered on top of it that has to be rebuilt.
