Two facts fall straight out of that table:

- **Hooks peak 51.0px, guard peak 85.9px — ratio 0.59.** They are separated by
  five slices (~30px, 10% of the axis) of 15px waist. That is not a lug on a
  ricasso, that is a second crossguard 59% the size of the first one, one blade
  width below it.
- **The blade proper is slices 0–23 — exactly half the weapon's axial length.**
  145px of blade against 146px of everything else. Max blade width 31.3px gives
  4.63:1 in projection, which agrees with your geometric 4.9:1.

Slot cost, measured as area removed at constant width: slice 15 is 29.3px wide
with 179 filled px; slice 17 is 29.6px wide with 142. The slots delete ~21% of
the blade's projected area, and at 64px the thumbnail renders rows 20–31 as
`##.##` — a two-rail fork, not a blade, over 12 of the ~29 blade rows.

## What I see

The silhouette does not read as a two-handed war blade. It reads as an oversized
dagger growing out of a thicket. Blade-only silhouette (`--isolate 1`) confirms
the mass is short and leaf-shaped; the long parallel-sided section you describe
in the header comment is only slices 8–23, and half of that is hollowed by the
slots. Zoomed into the hilt region the ricasso/hooks assembly reads as **antlers
or a tree branch** — 0.030 and 0.017 radius capsules with rounded caps are tubes,
not forged flat lugs, and the eye files them as organic growth rather than steel.
In the clay pass the mid-blade is **a flat plank**: one uniform value edge to
edge with a single thin darker strip at the rim. I cannot see a ridge, a fuller
trough, a shoulder, or a secondary bevel. The guard and grip in the same clay
frame show clear light/dark modelling, so the clay mode is not the problem — the
blade genuinely has no readable section. The tip is the one part that works: the
clip crease is a distinct line, the point sits off-axis, and the "crinkle" is
mostly ss1 aliasing on the LB→LC profile corner at (-0.116,1.26)→(-0.070,1.56),
not a form failure.

## Answers to the two things you flagged

**1. The hooks are the wrong idea, not the right idea badly sized.** 51 vs 86px,
five slices apart, both symmetric about the axis. Symmetry is the tell: any
bilateral widening within a blade-width of the crossguard will be read as a
crossguard, at any size you shrink it to. Shrink them to 30px and you get a
timid crossguard instead of a bold one; you do not get a lug. Scrap them. If you
want the memorable silhouette event back, put it somewhere with no competition
and make it **asymmetric** — a single spine spur on one side only, high on the
blade around y≈1.3, projecting ~0.055. Asymmetric events cannot be misread as a
guard, and the upper blade currently has nothing happening in it at all.

**2. Yes, longer and narrower, and framing is not your constraint.** Frame fill
is already 75.2% height at 35.0% width — the camera autoscales, so growing the
weapon costs you nothing but a slightly narrower blade in pixels.

## Biggest gap

**The ricasso-plus-hooks assembly consumes 28% of `bladeLen` and hands it to the
hilt, which is simultaneously why the blade is stubby and why there are two
crossguards. Delete it and reclaim that length as blade.**

It is one region, one edit, and it fixes both flagged problems at once. Right now
y=0 → 0.48 (the ricasso block plus the hooks) reads as hilt, not blade. So the
weapon's visual blade is y=0.42 → 1.72 = 1.30 units against a total visual length
of ~2.51 (1.72 + gripLen 0.44 + ~0.35 of pommel) — 51.8%, which is exactly the
50/50 split the slice table measured. Shipped two-hander hero silhouettes sit at
65–72% blade.

Concretely:

- Drop `dC` (both `horn` and `spur`) entirely.
- Shorten the ricasso to a stub: `P1_HEEL` 0.42 → 0.20, and shrink the `dA` box
  to a half-height of ~0.12 centred near y=0.09. Keep it — a short blunt ricasso
  above a guard is good and it gives the guard something to grip — just stop it
  being a 0.48-unit structural member.
- `d.bladeLen` 1.72 → 2.05, `d.bladeHalfW` 0.132 → 0.112. Retune
  `bladeHalfWidth`/`p1_profile` control points to the new heel and tip.

That yields a blade proper of 1.85 × 0.224 = **8.3:1**, and 1.85 / 2.84 total =
**65% blade**. Both land in shipped territory. The guard becomes the sole cross
event and the unambiguous widest point, and the negative space under it — which
rubric 1 asks to be deliberate — finally has a clean waist to sit against.

Two follow-ons, explicitly ranked below the above and not to be attempted first:
the slots need `hx` cut from `2.8*r` to about `1.6*r` (slotA's current half-width
0.106 against a local half-width of 0.126 leaves rails at 16% of half-width; 50%
is the minimum that survives thumbnailing), and drop to one slot rather than two
stacked ovals. And the section is flat in clay because `flr = 0.22 + 0.06*ux`
under `band = smoothstep(0.60, 0.53, ux)` clamps the inner 53% of the face to a
0.7° slope, erasing the 21.7° ridge roof and most of the 4.8° flat that
`p1_section`'s `min()` was supposed to produce. Narrow the fuller and harden the
shoulder — `smoothstep(0.31, 0.28, ux)` — so the ridge survives from ux 0 to 0.28
and there are four value bands spine-to-edge instead of one.

## What is already working

The clip point is genuinely good: the two-break left chain against the single
right sweep throws the point convincingly off-axis, and it is the only place on
this weapon where a shipped-art silhouette event already exists — build the
memorable moment around it rather than around the hooks. `dims()`'s wider guard
(0.345) and longer grip (0.44) correctly sell a two-hander. And the SDF
discipline is sound: convex half-plane intersection plus exact `min()` unions
means everything underestimates, the march is clean, and the 3s render cost is
unchanged — do not trade that away for a smooth-union approach when you rebuild
the heel.
